# Other languages (branch `i18n`, 2026-11-22)

**Before launch: no native speaker has read any of this.** Every string in `src/i18n.js` and every page under
`src/de/`, `src/es/`, `src/fr/`, `src/pt-br/` and `src/id/` was written by Claude. Have each language reviewed
by a native speaker before merging. The "Open questions" section below lists the wording choices most likely to
need a change.

## Why

remove.bg shuts down on 1 December 2026. Its site comes in 35 languages, and much of its traffic is from
countries where English is not the first language. goodbye.bg was English only. Translated pages give those
visitors a page in their own language when they search for a replacement.

## Which languages, and why these five

| Code | Language | Why |
|---|---|---|
| `pt-BR` | Portuguese (Brazil) | Brazil is about 8% of remove.bg's traffic, 4th by country. |
| `id` | Indonesian | Indonesia is about 9% of remove.bg's traffic, 3rd by country. |
| `es` | Spanish | Spoken across Spain and Latin America; remove.bg has a Spanish site. No single Spanish-speaking country is in the top 5, so this pick rests on the size of the language, not on a measured share. |
| `de` | German | remove.bg has a German site, and German is one of the largest languages on the web in Europe. Not a measured share. |
| `fr` | French | remove.bg has a French site. France, Belgium, Canada and francophone Africa add up. Not a measured share. |

The top countries by traffic that are left out mostly use English on the web: India (about 22%), the United States
(about 11%) and the Philippines (about 3.5%).

Sources:
- remove.bg's own language picker, read from the `locales` attribute in the markup of https://www.remove.bg/ on
  2026-11-22. It lists 35 languages: `id, de, en, es, fr, it, pt-br, ru, ko, ja, zh, zh-tw, pl, hu, th, da, el, uk, ms,
  vi, sv, tr, cs, ro, lv, fi, bg, sr, sl, lt, et, nl, no, hr, sk`. Each one is served at `/<code>`. All five chosen
  here returned 200 on that day.
- Semrush's remove.bg overview, last updated 11 September 2025, as quoted in a web search result on 2026-11-22
  (https://www.semrush.com/website/remove.bg/overview/). It gives India 22.18%, United States 10.98%, Indonesia
  8.81%, Brazil 7.96% and Philippines 3.52%. These are modelled estimates from a search snippet. The full country
  table is behind Semrush's paywall and was not read.
- A third-party stats page (aitools-main-fajlzj.laravel.cloud/tools/removebg/statistics) showed up in search with

Next candidates, in order: Italian, Polish, Turkish and Russian. Russian needs Nunito's Cyrillic file too:
self-hosted, so the CSP allows it, about 20 KB more. Japanese, Chinese and Korean are left out on purpose: the
page uses only Nunito's Latin file, it has no CJK glyphs, and the CSP rules out web fonts from other sites. Those
languages would draw in the system fallback font.

## How it works

- **The app page (`/`)** is one URL in every language. `src/early.js` picks the language at the top of `<body>`.
  It walks `navigator.languages` in order, stops at the first language we have, and stops at English if English
  comes first. Any `pt-*` gets `pt-BR`. `?lang=de` overrides it, for testing. `lang` is in `TEST_SWITCHES`, so a
  visit with it counts as test traffic. There is no switcher, and nothing is stored.
- **Strings** are in `src/i18n.js`, keyed by the English text exactly as it stands in `src/index.html` and
  `src/app.js`. A missing key shows English. A changed English string also shows English until its key is
  updated. `t("Frame {i} of {n}", { i, n })` fills in the slots. Each language also has its own quips: written
  fresh rather than translated word for word, with the ones that don't carry over left out ("Reticulating
  splines…" in all five).
- **The build** (`scripts/build.mjs`) inlines `src/i18n.js` into `dist/early.js`. That adds about 50 KB raw,
  13 KB gzipped, to a script the page already loads. It costs no extra request, and the service worker
  caches it like before. The build fails when a language lacks a key the others have.
- **No English flash, no layout shift.** In `index.html`, translatable elements carry `data-t`, `data-t-title`
  or `data-t-href`. early.js watches the parser with a MutationObserver. It translates each element once the
  parser has moved past it, which happens before that element can be painted. The smoke test reads the hero
  line in the first animation frame and checks CLS. On DOMContentLoaded a final sweep catches anything left.
- **Ad cards**: the label, the product line and the button are translated. What `/go` records stays the same
  (`l=ad` or `l=sponsored`), so the A/B data is unchanged. Zazzle itself stays English and in US dollars.
  The cards don't claim otherwise.
- **Messages from the server** (`functions/api/fetch.js`) and the HEIC worker are still written in English. The
  page looks each one up in the strings, and "The other site answered 404." has its number put back in.
- **Text pages**: `src/<lang>/remove-bg-alternative/` and `src/<lang>/how-to-remove-background/`, written by
  hand (`<lang>` is `de`, `es`, `fr`, `pt-br` or `id`). The URLs keep the English slugs.
  - Each page has its own canonical. The build fills `<!--%HREFLANG%-->` with seven alternates: `en`, the five
    languages and `x-default` (the English page).
  - The FAQ JSON-LD is translated too, so it matches the visible text.
  - Each path has its own `Content-Security-Policy: script-src 'none'` entry in `_headers`. `scripts/serve.mjs`
    only matches exact paths, so a wildcard would not be tested locally.
  - All are in `sitemap.xml`.
- The app's footer links to the text pages in the visitor's language (`data-t-href`). The text pages link
  to `/`, which shows the browser's language, not the page's. Someone reading `/de/` in an English browser gets
  the app in English. That keeps `/` a single URL, so no `?lang=` copies of it get indexed.

What stays English:
- the 404 page
- the web app manifest (name and description)
- download file names (`-goodbye-bg.png`)
- console output, crash and debug step names
- the server's own logs

## Claims

The translated pages make the same claims as the English ones, no more:
- free, high resolution, no account, no watermark
- up to 16 MP in Safari and on iPhone, 64 MP elsewhere
- up to 100 photos per drop on computers, 50 on phones
- up to the newest 50 photos kept on the device
- the Zazzle hand-off: our server holds the cut-out until Zazzle copies it; if Zazzle never does, it can't be
  opened after an hour and is deleted within a day
- the link fetcher keeps nothing
- remove.bg moving to Leonardo.Ai on 1 December 2026
- no API, an open source repo, rembg

When an English claim changes, change all six pages. `docs/seo.md` step 5 (update on 1 December) applies to
every language.

The UI names its buttons in each language ("Speichern", "Fotos wählen"). The how-to pages use the same words, so
they match what the visitor sees, as long as the browser is set to that language.

## Layout checks

Measured with headless Chrome and the page's real language detection (Playwright `locale`):
- languages: en, de, es, fr, pt-BR, id
- sizes: 390x844 (phone), 1440x900 (computer) and 844x390 (phone on its side)
- 0, 1, 3 and 12 photos, plus the background picker, the options row and the undo pill

Results:
- No page scrolls sideways.
- The empty page never scrolls.
- No button, label or card-bar control overflows its box.
- Download is at least 39 px wide in every language. That is the same as English: 12 photos on a phone give
  85 px cards.
- The card bar keeps its breakpoints. The Download label shows only on cards wider than 300 px, so long words
  ("Télécharger") have room.
- German uses "Speichern" rather than "Herunterladen" (13 letters).
- Every quip was measured in the working bar of an 85 px card and a 165 px card. Eight quips (de, es, id) wrapped to a
  third line at 165 px and made the bar 6 px taller, so they were shortened or dropped. Keep new quips at two lines.
- The ten text pages don't scroll sideways at 390 or 1440 px. The comparison table scrolls inside its own box, as
  it does in English.

The checker flagged only the background button's ring hint (35 > 32 px while it animates), and it does the same
in English.

The screenshots are not committed. To make them again: build, serve, then run Playwright with each `locale` at
those sizes. The hand-over note has the script.

## Open questions for the reviewers

- **German**: uses "du", which fits the playful tone. Business tools often use "Sie".
  - "Freistellen" is the trade word for cutting out. "Motiv" is used for the subject.
  - The Download button is "Speichern" (Save): check that it reads naturally for a file download.
  - The ornament ad says "Baumschmuck".
- **Spanish**: one neutral Spanish for Spain and Latin America, using "tú".
  - "Ordenador" (Spain) is used on the text pages, where Latin America says "computadora".
  - "Pegatina" (Spain) is used where Latin America says "calcomanía" or "sticker", and "móvil" vs "celular" has
    the same split.
  - Pick one market or split into `es` and `es-419`.
- **French**: uses "vous", "détourer" for cutting out, "Annonce" for the ad label and "Sponsorisé".
  - French spacing (a narrow no-break space before `?` and `:`) is used in the quips and the UI.
- **Portuguese (Brazil)**: uses "você" and "objeto" for the subject ("assunto" read oddly).
  - The quips lean Brazilian ("Fundo? Nunca nem vi.", "desapegar").
  - A Portuguese (Portugal) browser also gets this.
- **Indonesian**: casual, with "kamu" and "-mu". "Latar belakang" is spelled out everywhere, and "HP" is
  used for phone.
- **All five**: check the ad buttons. They are short on purpose, because narrow cards squeeze them.

## Adding a language

1. Add it to `LANGS` in `src/i18n.js`: `path`, `quips` and every key in `t`. The build and the smoke test name
   any key that is missing.
2. Copy `src/de/remove-bg-alternative/` and `src/de/how-to-remove-background/` to `src/<path>/`, then translate
   them. Keep `<!--%HREFLANG%-->` after the canonical.
3. Add the two paths to `_headers` and `sitemap.xml`, and add the folder to `TRANSLATED` in
   `scripts/test/smoke.mjs` and to the list in `scripts/test/xbrowser.mjs`.
4. Check the layout at 390x844 with 1, 3 and 12 photos, and with the options row open.
5. Have a native speaker read it.
